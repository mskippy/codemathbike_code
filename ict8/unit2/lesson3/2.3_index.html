Overview

Big idea: This is a synthesis project — you’ll bring together the design principles from 2.1 and the photo-editing skills from 2.2 and use them together in one piece of media.

Learning goals

  • Apply at least 2 design principles (contrast, alignment, hierarchy, spacing, colour & fonts) from 2.1.
  • Apply at least 1 photo-editing technique (Magic Eraser removal, Photopea clone/heal, or combining multiple photos) from 2.2.
  • Choose an appropriate creative format for your idea.
  • Export and submit correctly to Teams.

How This Builds on What You Know

Need a refresher? → 2.1 About Me · 2.2 Removing a Photobomber

  • 2.1 taught you design principles and AI image generation in Canva.
  • 2.2 taught you how to remove or edit elements in a photo.

Now you’ll combine both skill sets into one finished piece.

Choose Your Project (Pick ONE)

Pick ONE of the three project options below. Each combines a design skill from 2.1 with a photo-editing skill from 2.2.

Option A — Animated Promo

What you’ll make:

Take a designed image (yours or a new one), then add Canva animation, transitions, or a text reveal. Export it as a short MP4.

Steps:

  1. Build or select your base design
    Start a new Canva design, or reuse/adapt your 2.1 About Me poster as a starting point.
  2. Apply at least 2 design principles
    Check contrast, alignment, hierarchy, spacing, and colour & fonts as you build your layout.
  3. Animate your design
    Use Canva’s Animate tool to add transitions, a text reveal, or simple motion to your elements.
  4. Export your file
    Download as MP4.

Poster tip: Keep your animation simple and purposeful — motion should draw attention to your most important content, not distract from it.

Option B — Meme / Typography Poster

What you’ll make:

An edited photo paired with a strong type treatment as the core visual.

Steps:

  1. Choose and edit a photo
    Pick a photo and apply a photo-editing technique from 2.2 (Magic Eraser, Photopea clone/heal, or combine multiple photos).
  2. Build a typography layout around it
    Add bold text, a caption, or a meme-style layout that works with your edited photo.
  3. Apply design principles
    Check contrast, alignment, hierarchy, spacing, and colour & fonts.
  4. Export your file
    Download as PNG.

Poster tip: Make sure your text is easy to read against your photo — use contrast, an outline, or a solid text box if needed.

Option C — Album Cover + Liner Notes

What you’ll make:

A fictional artist brief — genre, mood, and artist name — brought to life with cover art and a short liner notes blurb.

Steps:

  1. Create a mood board / brief
    Decide on a genre, mood, and a fictional artist or album name.
  2. Build your cover art
    Combine a photo edit (from 2.2) with design principles (from 2.1) to create your cover.
  3. Write your liner notes
    Write a short blurb (2–3 sentences) describing the album or artist.
  4. Export your file
    Download as PNG.

Poster tip: Real album covers usually have one strong focal point — don’t crowd your cover with too many elements.
