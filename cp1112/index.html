Computer Programming 11/12

A combined, project-based course where you'll learn to think like a programmer and build real, working software using Python. Rather than everyone working through the same worksheet, you'll move through the BC Applied Design cycle — Understanding Context, Defining, Ideating, Prototyping, Testing, Making, and Sharing — on a series of projects that get more open-ended as the semester goes on.

This is a studio-style class: short lessons introduce new ideas, but most periods are independent or paired build time, with regular one-on-one check-ins rather than lectures. Whether you're brand new to programming or you've taken this course before, the pace and depth of your work will be matched to where you're starting from — and you'll be expected to push your own skills further as the semester goes on.

Units at a Glance

Based on daily 70-minute blocks over one semester (~19 weeks).

UnitWeeksFocus
Unit 0 — Launch & Diagnostic Week 1 Norms, environment setup, design-cycle intro, diagnostic placement
Unit 1 — Python Foundations Weeks 2–4 Core language fundamentals, at a pace matched to where you're starting from
Unit 2 — Guided Project 1 Weeks 5–7 First full design-cycle project: a console-based problem-solving tool
Unit 3 — Choice Project 2 Weeks 8–10 Data-driven app from a short menu, plus a surprise client feature request
Unit 4 — Independent / Specialization Project Weeks 11–14 Fully student-pitched project, tracked with your own sprint checkpoints
Unit 5 — Capstone Showcase Weeks 15–17 Major iteration or new capstone, fully documented, public/peer demo day
Unit 6 — Wrap-Up & Reflection Weeks 18–19 Portfolio curation, self-assessment, pathways connections
